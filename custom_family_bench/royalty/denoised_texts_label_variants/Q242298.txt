Princess Irene of Greece and Denmark (Greek: Ειρήνη, romanized: Iríni; 11 May 1942 – 15 January 2026) was the youngest child and second daughter of King Paul of Greece and his wife, Queen Frederica. She was the younger sister of Queen Sofía of Spain and King Constantine II of Greece. Early life

Irene was born on 11 May 1942 in Cape Town, Union of South Africa, where her parents were living in exile because of the German invasion of Greece during the Second World War. She was the younger daughter of Paul, Crown Prince of Greece (later King Paul), and his wife Frederica of Hanover. She had ten godparents, including General Jan Smuts, Lady Katherine Brandram (her paternal aunt), King George II of Greece (her paternal uncle), Queen Mary of the United Kingdom, and the Duchess of Kent (her paternal first cousin once removed). In 1944 the family moved to Egypt and returned to Greece in 1946 after the approval of the continuity of the Greek monarchy in the referendum with her uncle George II. Irene was educated at Arsakion school at Psykhikó Palace in Greece and at Schule Schloss Salem in Baden-Württemberg, Germany. During the 1981 Spanish coup attempt, Irene was at the Palace of Zarzuela with King Juan Carlos and her sister Queen Sofia; years later, she recounted the fear they both experienced when remembering the coup in Greece years earlier. In 2007, her biography Irene of Greece, the Rebel Princess was published. After the death of her father Paul, Irene became vegetarian.
